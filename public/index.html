    02 HOW IT WORKS · AND WHAT THE NUMBERS MEAN

    Turns

    Captions are cut into 20 to 45 second turns at sentence boundaries, so every claim cites a short, playable span. Pasted transcripts keep their speakers.

    Claims

    A model writes 6 to 20 standalone claims that keep names, numbers and hedges exactly as spoken, plus a one-paragraph summary.

    Evidence

    BM25 over the turns, five candidates per claim. In the benchmark ablation, lexical retrieval alone scored as well as hybrid lexical plus dense (F1 0.83 vs 0.81), so there is no vector index here.

    Judge

    Each claim is judged only against its candidates: supported, partially supported, unsupported or contradicted, with attribution checked and a verbatim quote. A forgiving parser records whether each verdict parsed cleanly, was repaired, or was scavenged.

    Operating point

    The same verdicts, two thresholds. Measured on the 1,064-claim planted-error benchmark: STRICT flags only unsupported, contradicted or misattributed claims (precision 0.997, 0.3% false alarms on clean claims, recall 0.44). SENSITIVE also flags partial support (recall 0.97, but 72% of clean claims get flagged). Moving the threshold changed the numbers more than changing the model did.

    Limits

    Auto-generated captions have no speakers, so attribution is only checked when the transcript names them. The judge sees retrieved passages, not the whole recording, so an omission is not an error it can see. Nothing here is medical or legal advice.
